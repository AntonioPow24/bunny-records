Many psychologists in Chimbote, Peru, work on their own: around ten patients a week, most of them online, and they handle everything else alone. The schedule lives in a notebook, payments in a spreadsheet, and reminders go out one by one on WhatsApp, whenever someone remembers.

None of that is serious on its own. Together, it shows: two appointments that overlap, a payment nobody chased, a patient who hadn’t been back in weeks without anyone noticing. Every day starts by checking three different places to know what’s next.

Existing tools don’t fit. Some are clinical record systems built for large clinics, with fields an independent psychologist doesn’t need; others are generic calendars that don’t understand a package of sessions or a payment by Yape.

Diván grew out of a conversation with the pilot’s psychologist: what she does every day, what slips through the cracks and what she doesn’t want an app to do for her.
